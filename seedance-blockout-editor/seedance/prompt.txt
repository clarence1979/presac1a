【Generation Goal】
Generate real, unedited live-action footage, 30 seconds, in a single take with no cuts, filmed by a real person with a real GoPro on a real FPV drone and handheld: a camera flight that starts on a trout leaping from a mountain river, crosses a ridge into a city of very tall towers, drops to street level through everyday life, and ends following a small fish up to the surface of the sea. It must look exactly like genuine footage posted by a creator, not like CGI, a 3D render, a video game or an animation. It should feel like a daring real drone-and-action-camera take through a real, living world, where every few seconds one clear thing happens right in front of the lens.

【Camera and Event Script】
Follow @video1 exactly for the camera; it is one unbroken move. Between the key moments the camera does not just fly straight: it looks around, panning left and right across the street life and tilting up at the towers.
0 to 3 s: tight close-up, long lens, on the rainbow trout as it bursts out of the river, body arched, tail flicking, water streaming off its scales. A bald eagle drops in from above with wings spread and talons forward, snatches the trout in a burst of droplets, beats its wings twice and climbs away from the camera down the river with the fish twisting in its grip, its back and white tail toward the lens. The lens widens and the camera rises to follow the eagle low down the valley over the river, its shadow sliding across the trees and the water.
3 to 7 s: the camera climbs over the forested ridge and dives between the skyscrapers, then sweeps in slow motion close past a lit corner office high on a tower. Inside, a man in a red sweater notices the camera, walks up to the glass, smiles and waves with his right hand raised, while behind him a colleague sits typing at a desk, eyes on the monitor, a coffee cup beside the keyboard.
7 to 10 s: the camera drops to a few centimetres above the avenue asphalt, ultra-wide lens. The skateboarder rolls straight at the lens, pushes twice with his right foot, crouches low with his arms out for balance, pops the tail and, in heavy slow motion, ollies over the camera with his knees tucked and his hoodie lifting. The camera tilts up to watch the grip tape, trucks, spinning wheels and the soles of his shoes pass overhead against the towers and sky. He lands behind the camera and rolls on.
11 to 14 s: still very low and ultra-wide, the blonde woman in the white summer dress walks toward the lens with a relaxed, confident stride, sandals on the asphalt, a small handbag on her shoulder. She glances down at the camera with a slight smile and steps over it in slow motion: seen from directly below against the buildings and blue sky, her left arm swings forward with the fingers of her hand spread just above the lens, her dress and hair moving in the breeze. Around her, ordinary street life: a man hailing a yellow taxi, a woman talking on her phone, a jogger passing, a small dog pulling at its leash, a cyclist weaving past the traffic.
14 to 17 s: the camera banks left around a corner into a side street, then right into the narrow alley, passing dumpsters, rubbish bags and fire escapes.
17 to 19 s: at the trattoria, under the striped awning and string lights, a waiter in a white shirt steps out of the door carrying a tray. The camera rises over a small table, slows, and zooms in on a plate of spaghetti with a meatball and tomato sauce, steam rising, as the man in the blue shirt twirls the pasta on his fork and lifts it toward his mouth; a glass of red wine stands beside the plate. Then the lens zooms back out and the camera moves on past two friends laughing at the next table.
19 to 21 s: low in the alley, a white cat crouched on top of a dumpster fixes its eyes on the camera, wiggles its hindquarters and springs, leaping over the lens in slow motion with its front paws stretched out, claws just showing, whiskers and belly fur passing right above the camera, tail trailing.
22 to 25 s: back on the avenue the camera races alongside a police cruiser, lights flashing, in a chase behind the red coupe, weaving past taxis. It holds for a moment in slow motion beside the open driver's window: the police officer, one hand on the wheel, leans out with a pink-frosted donut with sprinkles in the other hand and takes a big bite, crumbs flying, eyes wide on the car ahead. At the quay the red coupe hits a ramp and launches out over the water, wheels spinning in the air; the cruiser brakes hard, tyres smoking, bystanders on the quay turning to watch, and the donut flies out of the officer's hand through the window.
25 to 27 s: in slow motion the donut tumbles through the air, sprinkles scattering, turns to face the lens, and the camera flies straight through the hole in its centre and plunges into the sea in a burst of bubbles, just as the red coupe splashes down ahead.
27 to 30 s: under water, in shafts of sunlight over rocks and swaying kelp, a great white shark with its mouth half open chases a small silver trout; a whale glides far in the background and schools of small fish scatter. The trout darts upward with quick tail beats, the shark's snap just misses it, and the camera follows the trout up toward the bright surface, ending in a tight close-up on the trout exactly as it breaks through the surface, in the same framing as the very first frame.
