Why does this matter to you?

  • Most hacked accounts are not broken by genius coders. They are guessed from things people share about themselves: pets, teams, birth years, favourite things.
  • Those fun posts like "Your superhero name is your pet's name + your birth year!" collect exactly the clues an attacker needs.
  • Security teams do this kind of research (called OSINT, open-source intelligence) to find weak spots before criminals do. It's a real job.

Rules of engagement

  1. You may only attack this practice website. Mr Goh has given you permission.
  2. Never try to guess or use a real person's password or account. That includes classmates, family and teachers.
  3. We learn attacks so that we can defend against them.
In Australia, getting into a computer or an account without permission is a criminal offence. Real security testers always have written permission first.

Why the attack worked

  • The password was built from personal information that anyone could find online.
  • The same intel showed up again and again. A word and a number that keep repeating are the first things an attacker tries.
  • It followed a very common pattern: Word + Year, with a capital first letter.
  • A computer could rank and test guesses far faster than a person.

How Mr Goh should defend

  • Use a passphrase of 5 or more random words that have nothing to do with him, e.g. orbit-pencil-lagoon-maple-cactus.
  • Use a password manager so every account gets a different long password.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA), so a password alone isn't enough.
  • Think before posting personal details, and skip those "make your nickname from your pet + birth year" posts.
  • Never reuse the same password on different sites.
